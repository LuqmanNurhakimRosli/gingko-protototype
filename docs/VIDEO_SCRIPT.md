# Ginkgo — 3-minute pitch video: director's script

**Format:** a student founder pitches Ginkgo to a panel of urban experts, *Shark Tank* style.
**Length:** 3:00. **Half on camera** (you, your face, your words) · **half demo** (screen recording with your voice-over).
**Status:** script only. Nothing is generated yet. You record Part A and C on camera and Part B as voice-over;
send the files back and the demo footage, motion graphics and edit are built around your voice.

---

## The idea in one breath

> *"When a flood comes, people don't know when to leave or where to go. Ginkgo shows a city where floods hit,
> rehearses the flood hour by hour, and sends every family to a shelter that still has space — before the roads close."*

Everything in the video serves that sentence. If a shot doesn't, cut it.

**Tone:** a confident student talking to one smart, busy expert. Simple words. Short sentences. One idea per line.
Calm, not dramatic — the pictures carry the emotion; your voice carries the clarity.

### A3 · The problem — 0:18–0:52

> "Here's the problem. Three parts.
>
> **One:** we know roughly where floods happen — but the maps are made for experts, not for the planners and families who need them.
> In Selangor, **about one in four hectares** of land is high-risk, and **about 1.9 million people** live on it.
>
> **Two:** when the rain comes, warnings are too broad. People hear 'flood warning' — but not **when** to leave, or **where** to go.
>
> **Three:** evacuation centres fill up, and nobody sees it until people arrive at the door.
> On 26 December 2021, **more than fifteen thousand people** were in eighty-three centres in Selangor."

*(about 105 words.)* While you talk, numbers pop up on the right third of the frame (you stay on the left third):
- **24.1%** of Selangor's land is High susceptibility *(Ginkgo analysis, Normal rain)*
- **~1.9 million** people live in High cells *(estimate: Census 2020 × Meta HRSL)*
- **15,404 people in 83 centres**, 26 Dec 2021 *(news reports; see docs/REFERENCES.md)*

### A4 · Why now — 0:52–1:08

> "Cities like **Tokyo** and **Singapore** already warn people hours ahead, by neighbourhood,
> and show which shelters still have space. **We have the open data to do the same in Selangor.**"

*(about 35 words.)* Cutaway for 3 seconds: a simple card, two columns —
*Japan: 5 alert levels · live shelter crowding* | *Singapore: 1,000+ water sensors · SMS alerts* — then back to you.

### A5 · The answer — 1:08–1:20

> "So we built Ginkgo. It does three things:
> **Map** the risk, street by street. **Drill** a flood, hour by hour. And **guide** every family to a shelter with space.
> Let me show you."

*(about 35 words.)* Three icons slide in beside you as you say each word: **Map · Drill · Guide**.
On "Let me show you", look and gesture towards the screen (camera left) — that's the cut into the demo.

## Director's notes

### Camera (on-camera parts)
- **Frame:** medium close-up — head and shoulders, eyes on the upper third line. Sit on the **left third** of the frame;
  the right third stays clear for numbers and icons.
- **Lens:** phone main camera at 1×, never the wide lens (it stretches faces). Landscape, 1080p or 4K, 30 fps.
- **Height:** lens at eye level, about 1–1.5 m away, on a tripod or a stack of books. Lock focus and exposure (long-press on the face).
- **Look:** into the lens, not at the screen. Put a sticky note with a smiley next to the lens if it helps.

### Light
- Sit **facing a window** (daylight on your face), or a ring/desk lamp at 45° to one side, slightly above eye level.
- No bright window behind you. No ceiling light right above you (it makes eye shadows).

### Background and clothes
- Simple and calm: a plant, a bookshelf, a plain wall a metre behind you. Nothing moving.
- Solid colours. A green or white top matches the Ginkgo brand. No stripes or small checks (they flicker on video).

### Sound
- Quiet room with soft things (curtains, a bed, a wardrobe). Fans and air-con off while recording.
- Phone or lapel mic 15–25 cm from your mouth. Record **10 seconds of silence** in the room (room tone) at the start.
- Voice-over (Part B): the best free studio is a wardrobe full of clothes. Phone 15–20 cm from your mouth, slightly to the side.
- Save as **WAV or M4A, 48 kHz**. One file per scene.

### Performance
- Talk to **one** expert, not an audience. Smile at the end of A2 and at the very end.
- **Pause** for a beat after every number. Numbers need time to land.
- Do **three takes** of each part. The second take is usually the best; the third is your safety.
- If you stumble, pause, and start the **whole sentence** again — the editor will cut the clean one.

### Edit (what I'll build once you send the files)
- **J-cuts:** your voice starts a moment before your face appears (A1 → A2), and the demo picture starts under your last words of A5.
- **Captions** burned in, two lines max, Inter font, white on a soft dark band — many people watch without sound.
- **Motion graphics:** number call-outs (A3), the Japan/Singapore card (A4), Map · Drill · Guide icons (A5), highlight rings and
  zooms on the screen (Part B), the end card. Brand colours: green #08A045, water blue, Kikikuru purple for "evacuate".
- **Music:** light and hopeful, 90–100 BPM, royalty-free (for example the YouTube Audio Library). Low under speech (about −18 dB),
  up for one beat between parts. Soft rain ambience under the cold open only.
- **Colour:** warm and clean; the app's white 3D model on paper background sets the visual style.

### Honesty on screen
- Say **"drill"** or **"simulation"**, never "forecast" or "prediction". The demo shows a replay of a scenario, not live data.
- Every number on screen gets a small source line. Don't add news footage you don't have the rights to — the app's own footage
  and your face are enough.

## Shark questions to rehearse (30-second answers)

1. **"Isn't this what JPS and InfoBanjir already do?"** — They measure rivers and issue warnings. Ginkgo turns those warnings into
   *who* must leave, *when*, and *where there is space*. We want to plug into their river gauges, not replace them.
2. **"How accurate is it?"** — The risk map is checked against places that flooded in December 2021 (4 of 7 high, 2 of 2 hilly
   controls low). The drill is a rehearsal, not a forecast; the next step is to drive it with live gauge data.
3. **"Who pays?"** — Local councils and disaster agencies, as a planning and drill tool; later, insurers and developers for site screening.
4. **"What does it cost to run?"** — The map is static files; it runs on free hosting and even offline. The AI costs cents per
   question and repeated questions are cached.
5. **"What about privacy?"** — The "where am I" check stays on the phone. No personal data is stored.
6. **"What do you need?"** — One council partner, access to live river levels and the official evacuation-centre list, and a
   small team to pilot it for one monsoon season.
